Meet the Actronauts

This spec shows what Actronauts look like and how they behave, at work and at home: the Actronaut crew, how an Actronaut shows what it is doing, where it lives on your desktop, in your browser and in the native phone apps, what it may know about your phone, how you create one in Studio, how companies roll it out to managed machines, and the screens that make its safeguards visible: Rewind, mission preview, shareable proof, Guardian mode and initiative cards.

Everything below renders live with the production tokens, in both themes and at phone width. Use the theme and motion switches at the top to check both.

01

The Actronaut crew

Every Actronaut belongs to one crew of explorers. People recognize an astronaut, a pilot or a diver at a glance, so the crew is relatable from the first second, and no agent product dresses its characters this way. What makes them Actrone’s own is what they carry: a glass face, a lifeline back to you and charter lights on the suit. The same crew serves Work and Personal Actronauts.

1 2 3 4 5 6
  1. 1

    Glass face

    Every Actronaut’s face is dark glass with two bright eyes, whatever it wears. The eyes make it relatable; the glass keeps it clear of human likeness and skin tones.

  2. 2

    Explorer outfit

    Each family dresses for a frontier and a job: a spacesuit, a flight hood, a diving helmet, a caver’s hard hat, an inventor’s apron, a navigator’s coat.

  3. 3

    Lifeline

    Every explorer keeps a line to base: a tether, a radio cord, an air hose, a rope, a cable or a mooring line. It pays out when the Actronaut works away and reels it home when you say stop.

  4. 4

    Charter lights

    Three lights on the chest for on its own, ask and never. Ask holds amber while it waits for you; never holds red when your charter blocks it.

  5. 5

    Mission patch

    The Actrone mark rides as a small shoulder patch. Work Actronauts add the organization’s patch on the other shoulder.

  6. 6

    Receipt stitches

    Suit quilting and seams are rows of small marks, one for each signed receipt.

Six launch families

Each family dresses for one kind of job, but any Actronaut can do any job its charter allows. Owners rename their Actronaut; these are library names, screened against AI voice, model and assistant names and the US trademark register (the first draft’s Sol and Marin are OpenAI voices; Wilco, Grotto and Cairn gave way to registered marks). Launch ships 24 designs, adding a polar explorer, a mountaineer, a field botanist and more.

One face, every expression

Two eyes behind glass carry every expression: they widen to listen, look up to think, narrow when blocked and close to sleep.

Accessories carry personality

Owners personalize with accessories rather than colour, so every Actronaut stays inside the brand. Whether owners may tint a material is decision D7 in part 15.

Real astronauts and inventors, considered

Famous explorers would make instant characters, but their names and faces belong to them or their estates. The crew borrows eras and gear instead, which nobody owns.

OptionWhat appliesVerdict
Living astronauts and inventorsThe right of publicity: commercial use of a name or likeness needs consentNot without a licence
Historic figures23 US states protect the dead too, for up to 100 years in Indiana and Oklahoma. Estates enforce it: Hebrew University, which owns Einstein’s name and likeness, sued General Motors over an advertisement; CMG Worldwide licenses Amelia EarhartNot without a licence
An agent that speaks as a real personPuts words in a real person’s mouth, and content that resembles real people carries the EU AI Act’s labelling dutyNo
NASA insignia and mission patchesProtected; NASA does not endorse commercial productsNo
Eras and gear: 1960s spacesuits, classic diving helmets, early aviation, caving and workshop kitStyles that nobody ownsUsed

Directions ruled out

DirectionWhy not
Robots with antennas and dot eyesEvery agent product and toy uses them
Animal ears and creature headsRead as children’s mascots; critics already called Muse’s look infantilising
Plush and food mascotsMuse (Jolly, toast in sunglasses) and dots (a pear in glasses) already own this look
Logo-shaped figuresToo abstract to relate to; this spec’s second draft tried it
A single astronaut mascotPostman’s “Postmanaut” and others exist; the whole crew and its governance gear are what make Actronauts distinct
Photoreal human likenessesUncanny, and close to impersonation and likeness law
02

Presence states

An Actronaut always shows a real state; it never fakes being busy. Nine states cover everything, and every form shows the same nine. Colour appears only for the two governance states: amber while it waits for your approval, red when your charter blocks it. Each state also carries a text label, so colour is never the only signal.

Core form

2D character is the only character form: a vector rig drawn on the device and redrawn only when something changes. Core form is the helmet alone, drawn by the restyled Aura renderer that already exists. Runtime 3D was considered and dropped (part 03).

03

Presence tiers and what they cost

Characters are 2D only and never render on Actrone’s servers. The device draws a vector character and steps down to the core form on its own, so the platform pays for a download of tens of kilobytes and the device pays for a vector drawing.

TechniqueWhat it doesBudget it protects
Redraw only on changeThe renderer draws a frame only when the state, the gaze or the voice changes, and stops when the window is hidden or coveredIdle CPU under 1%
Automatic step-downBattery, thermal and accessibility watchers move the character to the core form and backBattery and fan noise
Voice sync from timestampsEye and helmet motion come from timing the speech service already returnsNo extra API calls

04

On the desktop and in the browser

On the desktop an Actronaut perches in a corner, on screen by default and out of the way. You call it by name, press the hotkey or click it. Closing a window never removes it; you can tuck it into the tray, where it still answers to its name. It steps aside while you share your screen and never covers the cursor’s work area. Without the desktop app, the Actronauts browser extension keeps it on in every tab whenever the browser runs, and Control Tower shows it in the page too. Further down: the desktop app working beside you, and the extension’s guards and tools (decision D26).

Placement: bottom-right by default, snaps to any screen edge, follows you across monitors. Size: 96, 124 or 160 px. Summon: its name, a hotkey or a click; a click opens the chat, a double-click opens the full panel. Driving: targets are addressed by role and accessible name (outlined in blue), never by pixel position, and any mouse movement pauses it. Stop: “Mabel, stop” or the hotkey halts input between keystrokes, offline. Visibility: on screen by default; hidden to the tray only by your choice, where its name, the hotkey and approvals keep working and calling it brings it out for that conversation. Closing the panel or Control Tower never removes it; quitting stops it on this computer only, and it keeps working in the cloud. Control Tower: the app’s main window, for every account, opened from the tray menu or a notification; the character stays outside it. Browser: without the desktop app, the character docks in every Control Tower page and, after a click, pops out above other apps in Chrome, Edge and Firefox on desktop while its tab stays open; Safari and phone browsers keep it in the page. Extension: with the Actronauts browser extension the character perches in the corner of the pages you allow and in the side panel, on whenever the browser runs, and from phase P2 it can act in your own tabs.

How the character thinks

The character has its own intelligence at two speeds. Reflexes on the device decide timing and gaze in under 100 ms; the brain decides meaning, writing stage directions into the same model call as each reply. It reacts to what you say and do, never to your face or tone of voice.

  1. It never reads you for emotion. No camera for presence, and no reading of your face or tone; the EU AI Act bans emotion recognition at work and in schools.
  2. Expression never pressures. “No” gets a neutral nod; no pleading, sulking or guilt.
  3. Governance outranks mood. No cheerful gesture plays over an amber or red hold, and it never mimes work that is not happening.
  4. A closed vocabulary. Only listed expressions, gestures and targets play; anything else is dropped.
14

Accessibility and brand checks

These hold on every surface in this spec and are release gates for the build.

  • Every state has a text label and an accessible name; colour is never the only signal.
  • Everything an Actronaut says is captioned; state changes are announced politely to screen readers.
  • Summon, approve, stop and dismiss all work from the keyboard; the stop hotkey cannot be remapped by a work policy.
  • Reduced motion swaps in the core form and removes all looping motion.
  • Touch targets are at least 40 px in renders and 44 px on phones.
  • Geist only, weights 400, 500 and 600; sentence case everywhere; radii 4, 6 and 8.
  • No gradients, glow or live dots; shadows only on floating overlays (bubbles, approval cards, sheets).
  • Chroma only for status: amber waiting, red blocked, green passed, blue for the computer-use target and info.
  • Icons are lucide at stroke 1.5; no emoji anywhere.
  • AI disclosure starts every conversation and call; no character is designed as a romantic or child-like companion.
  • Nothing that needs a passkey is approved from a Lock Screen, a widget, the watch or a notification; those open the app.
  • Every phone sense starts off and shows its last use; every screen read is on request and says it isn’t saved.
  • The Android bubble and the Dynamic Island show the character itself, never a dot or an indicator.
  • Browser warnings live in the side panel and the toolbar, which a page can’t draw over.
  • Meeting notes never start without the label and the consent line, and “no notes” stops and deletes them.

Decisions, accepted 2026-10-04

D7

May owners add colour to their character?

Section 8.1.1 governs Actrone surfaces but does not settle whether a customized character is a brand surface or the owner’s own content. Decided: the library ships monochrome, and owners may tint one material, treated as their content; amber and red stay reserved and can never be chosen.

D8

Where does the Actronaut live on the desktop?

Decided: on screen by default from the first run, in its own small always-on-top window that closing other windows never touches. The owner can tuck it into the tray, where its name, hotkey and approvals keep working, and it only comes back when called. Control Tower is the app’s main window, a separate ordinary window for every account, so the character never gets lost among console windows.

D9

How smart is an Actronaut, mind and character?

Decided: the mind runs on frontier models routed per phase, with your full context, a plan before acting and a check before it says “done”. The character has its own intelligence: reflexes on the device for gaze and timing, and stage directions from the brain for meaning (part 13). It never reads faces or voices for emotion and never uses expression to pressure you.

D10

How is the desktop character drawn?

Decided: natively. The Rust host draws the same Rive character files on the GPU through Rive’s open-source C++ runtime, so no browser engine runs while you are not using Control Tower. The presence core (states, reflexes and the stage-direction vocabulary) is one Rust crate, native on the desktop and WebAssembly in the browser. A phase 0 spike measures it against a web-view character.

D11

What is the desktop app?

Decided: Control Tower, 100%, the same console as the web app, every page and feature, for every account with the features its plan includes. On top of that, Actronauts get a richer home on the desktop: the character on screen, its name and hotkey, the tray and computer use on your own machine. Actronauts also work fully in Control Tower on the web, with the character in the page. The phone app is separate and shows only Actronauts, never Control Tower.

D12

How do Actronauts stay on in the browser?

Decided: a browser extension, Chrome and Edge first, then Firefox, Safari later. It keeps the Actronaut on whenever the browser runs, whatever tabs are open: the character in the side panel and in the pages you allow, its name, notifications and, from phase P2, acting in your own tabs under the charter. It shows only Actronauts, never Control Tower.

D13

How are Actronauts sold?

Decided: Actronauts plans, a product line of their own. You choose Work or Personal, then a plan; every plan includes the desktop app, the browser extension and the phone app, and nothing asks for technical setup. Sign-up to the first mission takes about five minutes, and one page installs the Actronaut on every device already signed in (part 06).

S1

Characters are 2D only

Decided: pixel-perfect 2D vector characters on every surface, with the core form for low power. Runtime 3D was dropped: at 96 to 160 px it reads as a flat picture, its shading is off-brand, and it doubles the art pipeline. Marketing can still use pre-rendered 3D films.

S2

Decided: the Rive runtime (open source under MIT, character files in kilobytes, a state-machine editor for the artist). An in-house SVG rig like the ones on this page stays the fallback.

S3

Senses are asked for in context

First launch asks only for notifications, places and Focus. Every other sense is offered the first time it would help, at most once a week each, and each row shows its last use.

N3

The Lock Screen never approves

The Dynamic Island, Live Activities, widgets, notifications and the watch show progress and ask, but anything that needs a passkey opens the app for Face ID.

N4

The Android bubble appears with work, not all day

It shows during a mission or while Mabel waits for you, and can be pinned to stay. This is the phone’s version of “on screen by default” (D8), tuned for battery and for small screens.

N5

The keyboard sends nothing until tapped

The draft appears in the keyboard; nothing reaches the app or the cloud until you tap Insert or Mabel’s key, and the keyboard never learns from what you type elsewhere.

N6

Mabel calls you only when it’s urgent

Calls through the app are for urgent approvals and Guardian alerts. Briefs and updates arrive as notifications or texts.

N7

Show what was seen

Select and ask, the Android assistant and the webcam each show a thumbnail or a line saying exactly what Mabel saw, and that it wasn’t saved.

N8

Browser warnings live in the browser’s chrome

The guards speak from the side panel and the toolbar icon, never from an overlay inside the page, so a hostile page can’t fake or hide them.

N9

Receipts say where the work happened

Every receipt is labelled “On this phone”, “On this computer”, “In the cloud” or “Both”, so charter rules such as “health stays on this phone” can be checked.
